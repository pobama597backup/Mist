## Quantum Computing: A Complete History (1980-2026)

The history of quantum computing spans from theoretical foundations to today's noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) era and beyond. In the 1980s, physicists like Richard Feynman and David Deutsch first proposed using quantum mechanics for computation [1]. The 1990s saw key algorithmic breakthroughs, including Peter Shor's factoring algorithm (1994) and Lov Grover's search algorithm (1996), demonstrating potential computational advantages [1].

Hardware development accelerated in the 2000s with various qubit approaches emerging, including superconducting circuits, trapped ions, and photonic systems. Major players like IBM, Google, Microsoft, and specialized companies like IonQ, Rigetti, and Pasqal emerged [1]. In 2019, Google claimed quantum supremacy with its 53-qubit Sycamore processor completing a calculation in 200 seconds that would take a classical supercomputer thousands of years [1].

Recent developments (2024-2026) show continued progress toward practical quantum advantage. Microsoft and Qolab defined scalable logical qubits for useful quantum computers in September 2026 [1]. NVIDIA established a center to link IonQ qubits with AI supercomputing, and Chalmers and Lund University tested quantum protein folding [1]. Pasqal reported 14% revenue growth with quantum services growing 34% [1]. Hardware improvements include 125 nanosecond two-qubit gates that advance quantum control [1].

## Key takeaways
- Quantum computing evolved from theoretical proposals in the 1980s to today's NISQ era with multiple hardware approaches and major industry players
- Key algorithmic breakthroughs in the 1990s (Shor's and Grover's algorithms) established quantum computing's potential advantages
- Recent progress focuses on error correction, qubit connectivity, and hybrid quantum-classical systems, with Microsoft, NVIDIA, and academic institutions contributing to scalable logical qubits
- Commercialization is accelerating, with companies like Pasqal showing significant revenue growth in quantum services
- The field is moving toward quantum advantage in specific applications like protein folding and optimization problems
